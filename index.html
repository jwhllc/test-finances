Key definitions
Chase cash ▼
Your operating checking account at Chase. This is immediately spendable — no phone call, no approval, no wait. The number you use for payroll and vendor payments.
Liquid reserves (MM accounts) ▼
Accounts 1005-7413 (EBR) and 8847-4912 (Yield) at Wells Fargo. These are money market funds — low risk, earning ~3.6% annually. Accessible within 1–2 business days by calling Kevin or submitting a redemption request. Not instant like Chase, but close.
Board-designated endowment ▼
Account 8805-0682. The board has designated this as an endowment — meaning it's set aside for long-term stability. It is not donor-restricted (no external legal requirement to keep it), but accessing principal requires a formal board vote. Think of it as a last-resort backstop, not operating cash. The investment income it earns does not require a board vote.
Operating runway / months covered ▼
How many months BRYC could operate at the current burn rate if no additional revenue came in. Calculated as: (Chase + MM reserves) ÷ monthly burn. Sector minimum: 3 months. NORI standard: 6 months. BRYC target: 8 months. BRYC's heavy revenue concentration in EBRPSS argues for staying closer to 9–12 months.
IPS spending policy (4% rule) ▼
BRYC's Investment Policy Statement sets a limit on annual withdrawals from the two MM operating reserve accounts: 4% of the 3-year average June 30 balance. This smooths out market swings so you don't withdraw too much in a good year or too little in a bad one. FY27 authorization: ~$105K. FY26 actual draw: ~$250K — a policy exception that should be noted in board minutes.
Conservative vs. optimistic pipeline ▼
Conservative = the lower estimate for a gift — what you'd expect if the donor gives less than hoped or the ask isn't fully successful. Optimistic = the upper estimate — best case. The gap between them is your uncertainty range. Use conservative for budgeting; use optimistic for goal-setting.
Earned YTD (investment accounts) ▼
The actual dollars the portfolio has earned year-to-date — pulled from Kevin's updates or the WF Advisors portal (Investment Results column). This includes both income (dividends) and appreciation (portfolio going up in value). This is different from what Molly journals. Molly records taxable income per IRS rules — dividends declared by funds, which is an accounting figure. Kevin's number is the real economic picture.
3-month buffer (forecast) ▼
The minimum cash position BRYC wants to maintain at any point during the fiscal year — 3 months of operating expenses. In the Forecast tab, months where projected cash drops below this line are flagged as fundraising urgency windows. It doesn't mean you're in crisis; it means you need revenue to close in that window.
Reminders and tips
Sync buttons require internet
The pipeline and expense sync buttons pull live data from Google Sheets. Pipeline data comes from the "Fundraising Revenue_Forecast_Planning" sheet (tab: No Names_FY27 Revenue). Expense data comes from the FY27 Operating Budget tab. They won't work if you're offline or if the Sheets API key has an issue. If a sync fails, the error message tells you what went wrong.
Data saves to this browser only
WF balances, Chase balance, advisor log entries, and pipeline data save to your browser's local storage. They persist between sessions on the same device and browser, but won't appear on another person's machine or if you clear your browser data.
Admin vs. viewer access
Admins can update balances, log advisor updates, add and delete pipeline entries. Viewers can see everything but not edit. Sign in with the Admin PIN to get full access. Share the URL with others who only need to view.
Keep the PIN private
The Admin PIN is set in the HTML file. Anyone who downloads the file can read the PIN, so share the live URL rather than the file itself. The current PIN is set to 2025 — change it in the file before sharing widely.
The "This Year" column in WF statements resets January 1
BRYC's fiscal year runs July–June. The WF statement's "This Year" column follows the calendar year (Jan–Dec) and resets every January. For fiscal year income totals, add up the "This Period" column across your July through June statements.

What this is
How to model cash runway and identify fundraising urgency windows for board-level conversations.
Step Item
1 Ensure pipeline and expense syncs have been run recently. Stale data produces misleading projections.
2 Click Forecast. Set "Months to model" to the horizon you want (e.g. 12 for a full fiscal year view).
3 Set "Revenue confidence" to reflect how certain you are pipeline gifts will close. 100% = close everything. 80% = close 80% of the pipeline.
4 Toggle "Use optimistic pipeline" on and off to switch scenarios. Run both for a board presentation — the range tells the story.
5 Read the "When do we need to raise?" table (bottom right). Each row is a month where projected cash drops below the 3-month buffer. These are your fundraising deadlines.
Note: A month in this table is not a crisis — it means revenue needs to close by then. Use it to drive ask timing.
6 The month-by-month chart shows the full trajectory. The red dashed line = 3-month buffer floor. Months where the green line dips below red match the raise table.
Watch out: The Forecast tab spreads revenue evenly across the period. In reality revenue is lumpy. Use the Pipeline tab filtered by quarter for a more granular view of when money is expected.
